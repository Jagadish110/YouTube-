In twenty twenty-two, Zomato bought his company. In twenty twenty-six, he became the boss of all of it.
This is the story of Albinder Dhindsa, and the three decisions that got him there.
He grew up in Patiala, in Punjab. He worked abroad as a transportation analyst, studying how people and goods move through cities, and later at the investment bank U B S.
In twenty eleven he came back to India and joined a young company called Zomato, to run its international expansion.
There he teamed up with Saurabh Kumar, an old colleague, who was working on a delivery idea for neighbourhood shops.
Albinder helped part-time. Then he quit Zomato to go all in. In December twenty thirteen, Grofers was born.
The idea was simple: you order online, and someone picks up your groceries from a local shop.
It grew fast. In January twenty fourteen, Grofers had four delivery boys. Fifteen months later, it had two hundred delivery partners.
Big investors like SoftBank, Sequoia and Tiger Global poured money in. In total, Grofers raised about six hundred and thirty million dollars.
But fast growth hid a problem. In January twenty sixteen, Grofers pulled out of nine cities.
For years after that, it was just another grocery app in a crowded market.
Then came decision number one. In twenty twenty-one, Albinder bet the company on speed.
Grofers built small dark stores close to customers and promised delivery in ten minutes. By November, it was handling about one hundred and twenty-five thousand orders a day.
In December twenty twenty-one, it changed its name to Blinkit.
But ten-minute delivery costs a lot to build. So came decision number two: he sold to his old employer.
In twenty twenty-two, Zomato bought Blinkit in an all-share deal worth about four thousand four hundred crore rupees.
Decision number three: he stayed. Many founders leave after they sell. Albinder stayed on to run Blinkit.
Under Zomato, Blinkit grew from three hundred and eighty-three stores to two thousand seven hundred and fifty-one by September twenty twenty-six.
At the end of twenty twenty-five, it made its first adjusted profit: four crore rupees. This year, it made one hundred and eighty-four crore in a single quarter.
Then, in January twenty twenty-six, Zomato's founder Deepinder Goyal announced he would step down as group CEO.
His pick to replace him was Albinder. Goyal wrote that he has the D N A of a battle-hardened founder.
So the man who once worked for Zomato, then sold his company to Zomato, now runs Zomato's parent company, Eternal.
Three decisions. Betting on ten minutes when it looked crazy. Selling to a stronger partner instead of fighting alone. And staying when he could have walked away.
