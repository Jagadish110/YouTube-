Fifteen stories, one video: from three bridges in Keev to the wheat fields in your village, in plain words.
Ten stories in depth first, then five quick hits. Let's begin.
Story one. Russia hit three bridges in the Ukrainian capital, Keev, in a huge overnight attack on Friday.
Ukraine's air force counted eighty four cruise missiles and four hundred and four attack drones. Twenty three places were hit.
The three bridges are the Podilsko Voskresenskyi, the Southern, and the Darnytskyi. One reopened by about three in the afternoon. One was still being assessed, and one was partly restricted.
Who is affected? People in Keev and other cities. Water, power and heating were cut in places, and the mayor asked people not to drive.
The number of dead is not settled. Early reports said at least three people were killed, and later reports said at least nine. Counts differ by source.
Why now? President Putin had promised a much more painful response after a strike on the Crimean Bridge earlier this week.
What happens next? No ceasefire has been agreed. European leaders meeting in Berlin on Thursday asked for a winter truce on energy attacks.
Story two. Poland says Russian missiles and a drone entered its airspace overnight into Friday.
The defence minister said it was probably two missiles and a drone. One was a Kh one oh one cruise missile. He called it a provocation.
Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the objects were Russian and it looked deliberate.
Debris was found on farmland near the village of Molodych. A loud bang was heard near a railway site in Rudnik nad Sanem. No damage or deaths were reported.
Air traffic controllers at Rzeszow airport were moved out for safety.
Why it matters: Poland is a member of NATO, the military alliance of Western countries, and it borders Ukraine.
What happens next? Investigations are under way at three locations.
Story three. The Congress party is asking the government for an all party talk on delimitation.
Delimitation means redrawing constituency borders and seat counts after a census. It decides how many members of Parliament each state sends.
The opposition's four point plan: keep the Lok Sabha at five hundred and forty three seats, keep each state's share, give women one third of existing seats now, and freeze this for twenty five years.
Who is affected? Every state, because each state's share of seats depends on it.
Earlier this year a constitution change bill linking women's reservation to delimitation failed in the Lok Sabha. It got two hundred and ninety eight votes, but needed three hundred and fifty two.
A change like this needs two thirds of members present and voting, so the government needs opposition votes.
What happens next? The Monsoon Session has stayed open since August, a record. A winter session is expected in December, but no date is confirmed.
Story four. The Railway Minister says India's first bullet train service will start in two thousand twenty seven.
Ashwini Vaish nau said it will begin between Surat and Vaapi in Gujarat. The rest of the Mumbai to Ahmedabad line opens in phases.
The project was announced in two thousand seventeen. Delays over land and permissions in Maharashtra, and Covid, pushed it back.
As of September, concrete piers stand along four hundred and fifty six kilometres, and viaduct is built for three hundred and seventy one.
In Maharashtra, a twenty one kilometre tunnel is being dug, including seven kilometres under Thane Creek, the country's first undersea rail tunnel.
The train will be India made. The first body frame of the two hundred and eighty kilometre an hour train is in structural testing.
What happens next? Track trials are expected by the middle of two thousand twenty seven, and then the first service.
Story five. The Supreme Court has allowed a lamp to be lit on a hill in Madurai, Tamil Nadu, on the twenty fourth of November.
It is the day of Kar tee guy Dee pam, a festival of lamps. The lamp is to be lit on a stone pillar on the hill, near a dargah.
The Court paused an order of the Madras High Court, and said this is a one time arrangement. It cannot be used as a basis to claim the same in later years.
Only authorised temple officials and government officers may go to the site. The public may not.
The conditions: no procession, no crowd, no slogans, no new structures, and no interference with the dargah or the mosque. Security must be adequate.
The Madurai Collector will arrange the lighting, and send a report to the Court within three days of the festival.
What happens next? The larger questions of religious rights are kept for a later stage of the case.
Story six. In Rajasthan, the Congress party is ahead of the B J P in village and district council elections.
These are the panchayat samiti, or block councils, and the zila parishad, or district councils.
In block councils, Congress won about three thousand seven hundred seats, and the B J P about two thousand one hundred and eighty.
In district councils, Congress won five hundred and ninety nine seats, the B J P three hundred and one.
Counting took place in thirty seven of forty one districts. Tallies were still moving, and a later count showed slightly higher numbers for both.
Why it matters: these councils run village development work. The Congress state chief asked the chief minister to resign, and the B J P said it will review the results.
What happens next? Counting in four districts, including Kota and Baran, is set for the seventeenth of December.
Story seven. The Bombay High Court is thinking about a full ban on selling and making firecrackers in Mumbai.
The two judges said the police are helpless against illegal bursting. They kept their order for later, so this is not yet a ban.
The court may allow only community displays at fixed grounds. Reports say the order could cover all of Maharashtra.
Why now? Reports say there were more than three hundred fires linked to crackers in Mumbai during Diwali.
A petitioner also says some so called green crackers carry fake codes and banned chemicals. The court asked about licences and green cracker claims.
Who is affected? Mumbai residents, who breathe the smoke, and the sellers and makers of crackers.
What happens next? We wait for the court's order. No date has been given.
Story eight. The Madras High Court has asked Tamil Nadu how it can drop a twenty seven thousand four hundred crore rupee airport halfway.
The planned airport is at Pah ran door, near Chennai. It was meant to be Chennai's second airport.
The state government said in August it would drop the project. Land of more than seventeen hundred acres has already been acquired, and over six hundred and eighty crore rupees spent.
The judges said an airport cannot be built, or dropped, on whims and fancies. They said studies must have been done before land was taken.
The state's lawyer said much of the land is water body or farm land, so green clearance would be hard.
A citizen filed the case. He says the decision is arbitrary and no expert report was shown.
What happens next? The court sent notices to the state, the Centre and the Airports Authority. The next hearing date was not stated.
Story nine. Farmers have sown less winter crop this year. Wheat sowing is down by about fifteen percent.
Winter crops are called rabi crops. By the thirteenth of November, they covered about one hundred and seventy eight lakh hectares. A year ago, it was about two hundred and one lakh.
Wheat covered fifty lakh hectares, against fifty nine lakh last year. Gram, or chana, is down more than twenty percent.
Why? The monsoon was thirteen percent below normal, so the soil is dry. Reservoirs and canals are below normal in key wheat states, and wheat needs several waterings.
Late rain also damaged some paddy and sugarcane, and delayed sowing of wheat and mustard. Some farmers had to sow mustard again.
The gap is shrinking, though. Two weeks earlier, total sowing was down forty six percent.
What happens next? The agriculture minister told officials to speed up sowing and urged crops that need less water, like chana, mustard and millets. The ministry says fertiliser and seed stocks are adequate.
Story ten. In Chhattisgarh, the government began buying paddy from farmers on the fifteenth of November.
Farmers are paid up to three thousand one hundred rupees a quintal. A quintal is one hundred kilograms.
That is the support price of two thousand four hundred and forty one rupees, plus a state bonus under the Krishak Unnati Yojana.
More than twenty seven lakh farmers are registered, and about three thousand one hundred buying centres are open.
Each farmer can sell up to twenty one quintals per acre. Slots are booked through the Tuhar Token app.
The state aims to buy about one hundred and sixty lakh tonnes this season.
What happens next? Buying goes on until the fifteenth of January. We did not find an official payment date for this season.

It is a first, and the training is due to begin in two thousand twenty seven.

It moved past the Nasdaq and the Hong Kong exchange. This is information, not investment advice.
