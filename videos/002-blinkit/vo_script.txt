Blinkit took almost twenty-one thousand crore rupees in orders in just three months. And for the first time, it's actually making money doing it.
So how does a ten-minute delivery app turn a profit? Let's break it down.
Blinkit started in twenty thirteen as Grofers, an online grocery app founded by Albinder Dhindsa and Saurabh Kumar.
For years it was a normal grocery app. Then in twenty twenty-one, it bet everything on speed: ten-minute delivery, and a new name, Blinkit.
In twenty twenty-two, Zomato bought it for about four thousand four hundred crore rupees, paid in Zomato shares.
Today, Zomato's parent company is called Eternal. And Blinkit is now its biggest business by far.
Here's the first thing to understand: the dark store.
A dark store is a small warehouse inside your neighbourhood. No customers walk in. It's packed with the few thousand things people order again and again.
Because the store is close, the rider only goes a short distance. The speed comes from where the store sits, not from fast driving.
Blinkit had two thousand seven hundred and fifty-one dark stores at the end of September. It wants more than three thousand five hundred by March twenty twenty-seven.
The second thing: Blinkit now owns what it sells.
In July twenty twenty-five, Eternal said Blinkit would start owning its stock, the way a supermarket does. So its revenue now counts the full price of what it sells.
That's why revenue jumped. From July to September, Blinkit's revenue was nineteen thousand one hundred and fifty-two crore rupees, up ninety-four percent in a year.
That's about seventy-nine percent of everything Eternal earns. The food delivery app that started it all is now the smaller part.
But revenue isn't profit. So where does the money actually come from?
Two main places. The margin on every product it sells. And brands paying to show up first when you search, which is advertising.
Here's the scorecard. A year ago, Blinkit lost one hundred and fifty-six crore rupees on an adjusted basis. This quarter, it made one hundred and eighty-four crore.
That's less than one rupee of profit for every hundred rupees of orders. Thin, but it has improved for six quarters in a row.
And in its oldest cities, the margin is already above five percent. That's the real signal: once a store matures, it makes money.
Thirty-seven million people now order from Blinkit every month.
So what's the catch? Every new store loses money at first. And Blinkit is opening hundreds of them, while Swiggy Instamart, Zepto and others fight for the same customers.
If the new stores mature like the old ones, Blinkit becomes a retail giant. If they don't, the profit disappears again.
